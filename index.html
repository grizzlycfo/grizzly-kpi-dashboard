✅
H1 2026 closes profitable at +$357K net income. The business recovered strongly from January's catastrophic production loss. Q2 profitability is real and sustained. Key risk remaining: subcontractor cost volatility and revenue lumpiness.
Critical Findings
🚨
January Production Blowout
January had $647K in subcontractor costs against only $268K in revenue — a 241% overrun. This single month cost the company ~$797K in net losses and nearly wiped out H1 profitability. A hard pre-approval rule for any project COGS exceeding 60% of billed revenue would prevent recurrence.
⚡
Revenue Volatility — 6.6x Swing
Revenue ranged from $223K (Apr) to $1.49M (Mar) — a 6.6x monthly swing. This lumpy project-based revenue makes cash flow unpredictable. Target building 30–40% of revenue from retainer contracts. Even $250K/month in stable MRR would transform financial predictability.
🔧
Subcontractor Rate: 37.1% of Revenue
H1 subcontractor spend was $1.51M — 37.1% of revenue. Industry target for a healthy agency is 20–30%. Outlier division drove $945K of this (62%). Evaluate whether Outlier's production model can shift more work in-house or renegotiate rate cards with top vendors.
📈
Q2 Turnaround is Real
Feb, Mar, May, and Jun were all profitable. Q2 net income of ~$96K proves the core business works when projects are properly scoped. Mar was exceptional at $679K net income on 45.5% net margin. The engine is healthy — it's project discipline that needs work.
💼
Outlier Division: Profit Leader
Outlier generated $1.55M in revenue but $307K net income (19.9% net margin). Grizzly Core generated $2.53M but only $50K net income (2.0% margin). Outlier is significantly more profitable per dollar of revenue. Consider replicating Outlier's operating model in the core agency.
📉
Payroll Collapse: Intentional?
Internal payroll dropped from $158K (Jan) to $42K (May/Jun) — a 74% reduction. This is dramatic. Either significant team reductions occurred or classification shifted from W2 to 1099 (which inflates subcontractor line). Verify this is intentional and won't create delivery risk as H2 revenue ramps.
🏦
Balance Sheet Recovering
Total equity swung from -$354K in January to +$800K by June — a $1.15M improvement. Line of credit is nearly eliminated ($504 remaining vs. $146K in Jan). Deferred revenue of $771K signals strong contracted work ahead. The balance sheet is in the best shape since year-start.
📬
Accounts Receivable Efficiency
AR swung from $825K (Mar) to $159K (Feb) to $780K (May) — collection cycles are irregular. Average DSO across H1 is approximately 27 days vs. an agency benchmark of 30–45 days, which is actually healthy. However, the May spike to $780K signals delayed invoicing or slow-pay clients. Implement a weekly AR aging review and flag any invoice over 45 days for escalation. Getting May's $780K collected by June (which it largely did — $365K remaining) shows the team can execute when focused.
⚖️
Break-Even Analysis
Monthly fixed costs (payroll, benefits, rent, insurance, tech, officer comp) average approximately $167K/month. With a blended gross margin of 36.5%, Grizzly needs roughly $458K/month in revenue to break even at the operating level. May and June both cleared this threshold comfortably. The danger zone is any month below $400K — which Jan and Apr both hit. Building a $500K+ monthly retainer base would permanently eliminate break-even risk.
🔭
H2 2026 Cash Flow Forecast
With $771K in deferred revenue already on the books, $1.24M in total cash/reserves, and 4 consecutive profitable months, H2 is well-positioned. Assuming May/Jun-level revenue (~$610K/mo) and controlled subcontractor spend (under 30%), projected H2 net income is $500K–$750K. Full-year 2026 net income would land between $857K–$1.1M. Key risk: another January-style production blowout would cut that in half. Cash runway at current burn: 7–9 months even in a zero-revenue scenario.
🏆
Outlier vs Grizzly Core: Margin Comparison
Outlier generated $1.55M revenue with $307K net income — a 19.9% net margin. Grizzly Core generated $2.53M revenue but only $50K net income — a 2.0% net margin. Outlier is 10x more efficient per revenue dollar. The gap comes from Grizzly Core's heavier subcontractor and overhead load ($945K in subs vs. $564K for Core). Applying Outlier's production discipline to Grizzly Core projects could unlock an additional $300–$400K in annual net income without adding a single new client.
🎯
H2 2026 Outlook
With $771K in deferred revenue already contracted, a healthy cash position ($1.24M total), and 4 consecutive profitable months, H2 is set up well. If May/Jun margins (47–53% gross) hold and no January-style blowouts occur, H2 net income could reach $500K–$800K, making FY2026 net income $850K–$1.1M.
